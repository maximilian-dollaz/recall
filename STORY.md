# The story of Recall

Recall was built by Max with Claude Code between 22 October and 5 November 2026, for a small team building two physical spaces: Frequency Wellness Lounge and Zero Proof Lounge, under True Nature Media.

Every quote below is Max's own words, from the chats where Recall was built. `…` marks a cut. Other people appear as their roles. The plain lines between quotes say what was built.

---

## 1. The problem (22 October)

> The minimum viable problem that this is solving on the smallest level is just that the Chief Experience Officer has projects and sources of inspiration that he wants to keep track of and needs a place to put them.
>
> … this is a brand-new company that's being formed with people who are still determining what roles they need to take … Everyone has their own sources of inspiration, ideas, past projects, and resources (like websites, books, podcasts, and videos) that all need to be organized so that they can be directed in the direction of our North Star.

> Let's, right now, keep everything in the product roadmap that's necessary, but remove anything unnecessary from the initial core piece functioning.

> One key point is that the data needs to be easily accessible on mobile.

*Built: a private library on [GBrain](https://github.com/garrytan/gbrain), with a small model on the Mac as its librarian.*

## 2. The team's inspiration, not one person's documents (26 October)

> The first problem that this is solving is that everyone on the team has ideas and inspiration that they want to bring to the project, but they don't have a single place to store them where they can find them and take action on them.
>
> For context, [the CEO] … likes to take pictures wherever she goes of things that she wants to include in the physical spaces.

> The problem is that great ideas go into a notebook or go into a Google Doc or a notes folder, or inspiration is captured but never acted upon. That's what we need to be building, and that's what we need to keep in mind while we're building

## 3. If it can't be found, nothing else matters (26 October)

> On the point of saving something, if it's not able to be recalled, then the whole tool doesn't even matter.

The first test was a real one. The CEO, months later, with an interior designer:

> "Find me that picture of the pillow with the Middle Eastern design that I took around October"

> I want you to hold me accountable for ensuring that this tool is useful and not fancy. I shouldn't be coming from the place of being impressive or proving anything. It should come from the place of just raw utility.

> once that's done as in, I can actually use it on my phone, and I can have her install it and use it on her phone, everything else that we do from there is cherry on top, and we'll just continue shipping and shipping and shipping

*Built: one box to save anything, pictures described by the local model, search by description. The pillow test passed.*

## 4. The tree (26 October)

> I want it to resemble a tree where you have the roots pulling nutrients up into the trunk. … The roots are connected to reality and are pulling nutrients (aka context) from reality into the trunk. Then the trunk splits into major branches. Those branches split into smaller branches … and those branches split into leaves. Context gets pulled from life, from wherever it is recorded, up into the system and then dispersed into smaller and smaller branches.

## 5. What it's for, why you saved it, when it comes back (27 October)

> I think what, why, and when are going to be more necessary: what it's for, why you saved it, and when to resurface. … A sentence each is plenty.

> Sometimes you take a photo or you write something down, and then you, at that moment, know what it's going to be used for. In the moment of recall, you may not remember exactly how you want it to be used … That acts as a sorting function, and it also acts as extra context around the artifact.

## 6. Sorting disappears (27 October)

> The categories should mostly happen behind the scenes. … The idea is you take a picture or you have a moment of inspiration, and you're able to put it into the system and have it sorted with a very small description about why you took the picture.

> the librarian is able to create categories on its own, shuffle categories, delete categories, and create new categories. … As long as recall happens effectively, it doesn't necessarily matter how the categories happen

> this project is called "recall"

*Built: projects that form from what people say, a project board with every asset at once, and "brought back for you" on the day you asked.*

## 7. "It gets lost. It needs to be found." (30 October)

> I record something in my notes app, in a google doc, in notion, in a memoket memo, or in otter, and it's relevant to a project I'm working on, or it's something I need to take action on, or something I need to remember but it never gets sorted anywhere and it never gets revisited. it gets lost. It needs to be found.

> I want these learnings to be resurfaced so that I'm taking my own advice. That's the key. Taking my own advice

> I'm thinking there might be a possibility to have categories split and merge, kind of like cells, like living cells.

*Built: topics inside each project that form, divide and merge on their own, like cells.*

## 8. Human first (31 October)

> As a principle, this needs to be human first, keeping in mind that our minds, our neural circuitry, rewires so much slower than software is able to be adapted. We need to adapt software to how our neural circuitry already works.
>
> I want to integrate sacred geometry into design.
>
> Speak-to-text needs to be everywhere.
>
> How does the mind recall information that it needs to bring back to the surface?

> a core tenet of this project is to bridge the gap between idea and execution.

> When something goes in, you can determine whether or not it will stay private or if it will be accessible by a larger model or by an agent. That way, synthesis is able to take place across everything that is public, and it doesn't include that which is private.

## 9. Why it matters (31 October)

> integration happens through repetition. only that which continuously gets impressed upon the subconscious becomes part of who you are … those are the moments that must be repeatedly impressed upon the subconscious, repeatedly brought up and shown and expressed to you over and over so that you remember: oh yeah, that's who I am.

> it's always random companies that get my attention by notifying me on my phone. what if the highest version of myself was notifying me all of the time. It's taking back control in what is imprinted upon my mind.

> input process output. what's getting input? it should be my highest and best thoughts and ideas and action items

## 10. Ship it to the team (2 November)

> We're going to make this the objective of the day: to just ship this to 100% working.

> our team is sharing inspiration in our WhatsApp group when they could be just as easily sharing everything to a board that everyone can see … [A teammate] said that she uses a combination of Pinterest, Cosmos.so, and Are.na, and then brings it together in FigJam or Canva. So that validates the problem

> "What is this? What's it for, and why do you love it?" … loving it is also a sorting mechanism for whether or not you even record something in the first place. If you don't love it, then why should it be a part of this project?

> I like the way that we did this by shipping first and then editing from a real-world perspective because it's just easier for me to mentally process it.

*Built: Google sign-in from one team link, a phone shortcut that asks those three questions and listens, saving from the Mac with one key, and the team board.*

## 11. Non-resistance and the monkey (2 November)

> as an absolutely fundamental building principle for every piece of software that I build, the principle is non-resistance. … desire + non-resistance = desired outcome. If we can bake non-resistance into how we build, how we design systems, and how we think about designing systems, then we will attain the ultimate goal: the ultimate vision of idea to execution, as fast as possible with the least amount of friction possible.

> finding and addressing the monkey in every project is extremely important. … it's the one piece that, if it doesn't work, then nothing works, and it may change throughout the project as things go on.

> Privacy page is fine. Go live, baby.

## 12. Creating is remembering (2 November, overnight)

> Anything that's saved should either be auto-sorted into a project, or a project should be created if it's a new category, or it should exist in a category within an existing project, or it should go into the Clarify tab

> there are beautiful blue hues and beautiful tans and beiges, and the design is elevated. … It just feels natural. It is magnificent to look upon. It just feels good, and it registers as truth in the mind.

> I want you to use different agents as a product manager agent, an engineer agent, a UI agent, and a UX agent to redman this entire project … you own this objective … so that when I wake up in the morning, I am met with Recall - "creating is remembering"

*Built overnight by parallel agents: the river-delta look, boards that fill the screen, Clarify, delete with undo, and the team board as a tree with faces.*

## 13. Boards you create from (3 November)

> When it comes to copy, I want you to use Ogilvy's principles of absolute and utter simplicity, clarity, and conciseness. … it's not clever, it's not cute, and it is only absolutely 100% direct.

> Imagine you are Marie Kondo turned into a UX designer. That's how organized and simple I want this to feel.

> I can drag and drop multiple assets and put them into a chat window. Then I can, in a stream of consciousness, say, "Okay, this is what I want to create. Please create me this image," and then it will auto-generate an image … in real time.

> Carpets red, walls concrete, lights dimmer and more yellow
>
> Holy shit, yes.

*Built: the Dream board, Creator mode (pictures and words from a board into a new image), and invites.*

## 14. Going live (4 November)

> Please make it possible for people who are not on the True Nature team to log in and make accounts, because I'm going to send this to a lot of friends.

> Starting from the very first principle: what is being sent to them? How is it being sent to them? … It needs to have enough context that people understand what it is, why it exists, what it's useful for.

> we need the sorting mechanism to be able to chunk large documents.

> any data that is coming in and wants to come into the context library is able to do so autonomously, with as little human input as possible.

*Built: long documents and meetings read in full, a part at a time, each idea, action and decision sorted on its own and linked back to its spot.*

## 15. Open (5 November)

> I want to open source this project. So that researchers, users, entrepreneurs, and anyone can access the repository, see the entire project, and understand the entire project

> all of my projects going forward are going to be open source.

## The principles, in one place

- **Non-resistance.** Desire + non-resistance = desired outcome.
- **The monkey.** Find the one piece that, if it fails, nothing works, and solve it first.
- **Useful, not fancy.** Raw utility over being impressive.
- **Human first.** Adapt the software to how the mind already works.
- **If it can't be recalled, it doesn't matter.**
- **Nature as the model.** A tree for shape, streams for saving, a watershed for sorting, a reservoir for keeping, a delta for long documents, cells for topics ([docs/nature.md](docs/nature.md)).
- **Sorting happens behind the scenes.** People say what it's for; the system does the rest.
- **Private by default.** Sharing is a choice, per item.
- **Ship first, refine live.**
- **Plain words.** Not clever, not cute, only direct.
- **Bridge the gap between idea and execution.**
